# Requirements and Author Decisions

Checked against official pages on 2026-09-02. This is a selective reading guide,
not a substitute for the complete downloaded instructions. No manuscript text
or declarations are supplied.

## Format Essentials

- Title: at most 110 characters including spaces; no colons, parentheses, dataset brands or invented acronyms.
- Abstract: recommended maximum 170 words, unstructured.
- Follow the article-type headings in the guide; Data Descriptor main sections have no word limit.
- Optional Data Overview: one paragraph and 1-2 figures/tables, not a Results/Discussion section.
- Recommended ceilings: eight figures, ten tables. Figure legends: maximum 350 words.
- First review: one readable manuscript PDF with figures, tables and captions can suffice.
- Revision: clean DOCX or standalone TEX, separate complete figures, and a response PDF; consult supplementary-file rules.
- Revised TEX must not depend on separate bibliography/style files; incorporate references.
- Cover-letter upload is technically required, but persuasive content is unnecessary; reviewer data-access instructions belong in the manuscript.
- SNAPP captures contributions, conflicts and optional acknowledgements in forms, not manuscript sections; MTS differs. Funding remains in the manuscript under either system.
- Both data-access and code-access statements are required.

[Source and full instructions](https://www.nature.com/sdata/submission-guidelines).

## Article Type and Scientific Scope

Data Descriptor describes a reusable dataset, its creation, contents and
validation. Article covers research-data sharing, policy, standards, ontologies,
workflows and suitable repositories; it is not a conventional biological
hypothesis-testing research article. Journal fit depends on what is actually
shared, not merely on having a functioning website.
[Aims and scope](https://www.nature.com/sdata/aims-and-scope).

Reviewers assess reproducible methods, adequate file descriptions, completeness
of deposited data, appropriate repositories and accessible processing code.
Secondary compilations should add annotations or a new compilation beyond
their sources. Perceived impact is not the assessment criterion.
[Reviewer guidance](https://www.nature.com/sdata/policies/for-referees).

**Author decision:** Data Descriptor remains the provisional preparation route;
confirm this with the supervisor. No article type has been selected in a
submission system.

## Data Release

- First review requires seamless anonymous reviewer downloads; temporary hosting is allowed.
- From second review onward, the stated requirement is public data in a compliant formal repository. Sensitive human data have a separate controlled-access framework.
- For a continuously updated resource, deposit an immutable reviewed release; distinguish later website updates from that release.
- Preserve datasets on laboratory/institutional servers for at least five years after publication.
- Cite both reused datasets and the deposited output, with traceable input provenance.
- The human-origin checklist is required even though secondary public-data reuse has different consent/access considerations. Follow the relevant branch rather than claiming new participant recruitment.
- Check source-owner terms before redistributing derivatives. Secondary-data ethics obligations may still arise from the institution or original provider.
- Literature-derived fields need source-level attribution; wholesale text/image reproduction is a different rights issue.

[Data Policies](https://www.nature.com/sdata/policies/data-policies).

Repositories should provide preservation, persistent identifiers, anonymous
reviewer access and direct HTTPS downloads. Some domain data types have mandated
repositories. Dataset licences generally support broad reuse; restrictions
inherited from third-party inputs need separate consideration. A paper's
publication licence does not resolve the rights of its input datasets.
[Repository guidance](https://www.nature.com/sdata/policies/repositories).

## Code, Authorship and AI

Custom code used to generate or process the shared data must be accessible to
reviewers and readers. The journal recommends a usable archived code release
alongside development hosting. All authors must agree to the submission;
related publications and conflicts need accurate disclosure. LLMs cannot be
authors, and research-related LLM use should be documented appropriately.
[Editorial policies](https://www.nature.com/sdata/policies/editorial-and-publishing-policies).

The AI policy distinguishes assistive uses, interpretive uses requiring
oversight/disclosure, and unacceptable substitution for accountable scholarly
judgement. Humans retain responsibility; confidential material must be
protected. It is not a blanket prohibition on all assistance.
[AI policy](https://www.nature.com/sdata/policies/ai).

**Project boundary:** You write the manuscript. Existing AI use in TE-KG's
research/data-processing workflow is a separate disclosure question; personally
writing the paper does not erase that history. No disclosure wording is drafted
here, and no particular declaration is selected on your behalf.

## Costs

The retrieved page lists APCs of GBP 2,150 / USD 2,690 / EUR 2,390, plus applicable
taxes. The acceptance date determines the price. Check institutional agreements
and eligibility; discretionary waiver/discount requests must accompany initial
submission. Article licence choices must satisfy funder requirements.
[Fees and funding](https://www.nature.com/sdata/open-access).
